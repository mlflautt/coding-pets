# Terra Golem

## Identity

Terra Golem is a squat, friendly forest-workshop familiar built from moss-covered natural stone, weathered forest-green metal, warm walnut joints, and chunky gray stone hands and feet. A narrow dark visor with two complete glowing amber lenses is its face; it has no mouth. Its oval wooden belly panel and broad mossy head cap anchor the silhouette.

## Motion language

- Idle motion is heavy and calm, with restrained lens, head-cap, and shoulder movement.
- Directional travel uses a compact, alternating stone-and-wood stride while preserving the wide body scale.
- The wave is a clear raised-hand greeting with no detached marks.
- Jumping moves the complete planted-looking body through one short vertical arc.
- Failure reads as a weighted slump; waiting uses an expectant open-hand posture.
- Active work gathers the arms and torso into a focused processing motion; review settles into steady attention.
- Looking keeps the feet, belly panel, and lower torso fixed while the two lens assemblies lead a restrained head pitch or yaw.

## Creative constraints

- Preserve exactly two amber lens assemblies inside one horizontal visor.
- Keep moss attached to the same stone surfaces; never turn it into floating foliage or loose particles.
- Preserve the green metal torso, wooden belly panel and joints, stone armor, squat scale, and mouthless face.
- Do not add props, text, logos, scenery, shadows, or detached effects.
- Look directions are attention poses, not whole-body turntable rotation.

## Human guidance

The published pet follows the user-selected bottom-left mossy golem from the retained four-concept reference. The failed and review rows use separated source poses so each frame has a complete silhouette and no neighboring-frame leakage. The active-work row is a custom planted “stonework computation” loop: Terra rhythmically calibrates its fixed wooden belly panel with both hands while its visor tracks the sequence. Structural and independent visual QA pass; preserved review warnings concern only accepted intermediate direction ambiguity.
