# Terralisk

## Identity

Terralisk is a compact graphite salamander-gecko familiar with large amber eyes, cyan-and-amber living circuitry, translucent vent-like heat-fin gills, clawed feet, and a tight spiral tail. Its dark plated body and luminous biological details should remain organic and creature-like rather than becoming a conventional robot.

## Motion language

- Idle motion is alert but grounded, led by small head, eye, gill, and tail adjustments.
- Directional travel uses a low, agile quadrupedal scuttle; the leftward cycle is the exact framewise mirror of the approved rightward gait.
- The wave rises into a clear open-claw greeting without introducing a prop or detached effect.
- Jumping compresses and extends the full body through a compact leap while the spiral tail remains attached.
- Failure reads as a heavy wilt and collapse; waiting uses curious, increasingly expectant gestures.
- Active work brings the forelimbs and focused gaze inward; review alternates attentive head and hand poses.
- Looking keeps the grounded body and spiral-tail base stable while the head, neck, eyes, and heat-fin gills trace the 16 physical directions.

## Creative constraints

- Preserve the graphite plated body, two large amber eyes, cyan-and-amber circuitry, translucent gill fins, and single spiral tail.
- Keep luminous circuitry embedded in the creature; do not turn it into detached sparks, text, or interface graphics.
- Preserve the compact salamander-gecko proportions and clawed limbs across all states.
- Do not add scenery, props, logos, shadows, extra limbs, or detached tail segments.
- Look directions are physical head-and-neck poses, not eye-gaze substitutions or whole-body turntable rotations.

## Human guidance

The published pet follows the approved Terralisk concept reference retained with the package. Structural validation, three-reviewer blind cardinal QA, labeled direction review, and independent final visual QA pass. Subtle horizontal cues at 135, 157.5, and 337.5 are retained as documented minor warnings because the ordered normal-size loop resolves them without reversal or visible snap.
