# Chromia

## Identity

Chromia is a low three-quarter-view cuttlefish familiar with a plump oval mantle, one prominent sapphire-blue eye plus a subtle far eye, vivid coral-red and orange skin densely flecked with turquoise, yellow, pink, and violet chromatophores, a purple scalloped fin skirt, and a balanced forward fan of short curling arms. Its smooth underside has no dangling rear tentacle.

## Motion language

- Idle motion is buoyant and observant, using gentle mantle breathing, eye changes, fin movement, and small arm adjustments.
- Directional travel is a compact swimming glide with the mantle trailing the eye and arm crown.
- The wave lifts and curls one attached arm without marks, bubbles, or detached effects.
- Jumping reads as a playful hover pulse through whole-body elevation and compression.
- Failure dims the chromatophores and closes the eye before recovering.
- Waiting gathers the arm fan into an increasingly expectant posture.
- Active work uses focused eye movement and purposeful chromatophore color cycling rather than literal running.
- Review alternates attentive eye, mantle, and arm poses.
- Looking rotates the complete cuttlefish naturally around its vertical axis: rear/up at 000, right profile at 090, frontal/down at 180, and left profile at 270.

## Creative constraints

- Preserve the coral-orange painterly mantle, multicolor chromatophores, sapphire-blue eye, purple fin skirt, and short attached arm fan.
- Keep the silhouette unmistakably cuttlefish-like rather than turning it into a frontal octopus, toy, plush character, or hardware object.
- Preserve the clean underside with no dangling rear tentacle.
- Do not add shells, props, logos, text, bubbles, shadows, scenery, or detached effects.
- Direction poses are coherent physical creature turns; never substitute pupil-only gaze or arbitrary whole-cell rotation.

## Human guidance
